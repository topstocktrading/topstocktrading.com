Why Costco Earnings Matter More Than You Think

Every quarter, Costco gives the market something priceless: a ground-level view of what the American consumer is actually doing. Not what they're saying in sentiment surveys (which are at 47.8, near multi-year lows), but what they're actually spending money on. When Costco reports on Thursday after the bell, that's the data point that either validates or challenges the economic narrative the market has been building.

This quarter matters more than usual. The Fed just hiked. Inflation is still elevated. Consumer sentiment is near lows. And yet the NY Fed's household spending survey suggests consumers are still planning to spend. Costco earnings will be the tiebreaker.

What Analysts Expect

Wall Street is projecting earnings per share of $6.51 for Q4 fiscal 2026, up 10.9% from $5.87 in the year-ago quarter. Revenue consensus is approximately $82 billion. Costco has beaten EPS estimates in three of its last four quarters, so the bar is real but not impossible.

For the full fiscal year 2026, analysts project EPS of $20.42 — up 13.5% from fiscal 2025. The long-term growth trajectory remains intact. The question is whether macro headwinds are starting to show up in the near-term numbers.

Analyst Consensus

19 analysts rate COST a Strong Buy, 4 say Moderate Buy, 12 say Hold, and 1 rates it a Strong Sell. Average price target: $1,100.69 — implying roughly 17.7% upside from current levels. The Street is bullish but not euphoric.

The Three Numbers That Will Move the Stock

  1. Comparable sales growth — the most important single number. This tells you if existing Costco locations are doing more or less business than a year ago. Any number above 4% will be seen as a positive sign for the consumer.
  2. Membership fee revenue — Costco's membership model means this number is the purest signal of consumer willingness to pay for access. Membership revenue is high-margin and sticky. Growth here tells you about brand loyalty, not just spending.
  3. Management commentary on guidance — the forward-looking language matters as much as the reported numbers. If management sounds cautious about the consumer heading into the holiday season, that's a sector-wide signal.

The Read-Through for Other Stocks

Costco doesn't move in isolation. A strong print — especially strong comp sales and positive guidance — is a green light for the entire consumer sector. Watch XLY, WMT, and TGT for sympathy moves Friday morning. A weak print or cautious guidance confirms the UMich sentiment story and puts defensive consumer names (XLP, PG, KO) in focus as the rotation trade.
