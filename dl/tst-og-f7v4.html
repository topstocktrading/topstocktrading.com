Options Greeks Simplified
Delta, theta, gamma, and vega in plain English with real trade examples
The Greeks measure how an option's price changes in response to different factors. You don't need formulas — you need to understand what each Greek means for your actual trade.
1Delta — How Much the Option Moves
What it is
Delta tells you how much the option gains for every $1 the stock moves. Delta 0.50 = option gains $0.50 for every $1 the stock rises.
DeltaMeaning
0.90+Deep in-the-money — moves almost like the stock
0.50At-the-money — balanced between ITM and OTM
0.20 or lowerOut-of-the-money — mostly time value, moves slowly
2Theta — Time Decay
What it is
Theta is how much value the option loses each day from time passing. Every day you hold, it is worth slightly less — all else equal.
⚠ Theta accelerates near expiration
Options lose value slowly at first, then rapidly in the last 30 days. Weekly options give you very little time for the trade to work. Theta works against buyers and for sellers.
3Gamma — Delta Acceleration
What it is
Gamma measures how fast delta changes as the stock moves. High gamma (near expiration, near strike) means your option can move very fast — in both directions.
4Vega — IV Sensitivity
What it is
Vega measures how much the option changes for every 1% move in implied volatility. High IV = expensive options.
IV Crush
After earnings, IV typically collapses — even if the stock moves in your direction. Buying options into earnings often loses despite being right on direction.
