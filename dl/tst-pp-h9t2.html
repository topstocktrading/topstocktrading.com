The Trader's Pattern Playbook
The 6 chart patterns that appear most often with visual examples and exact entry rules
These 6 patterns appear constantly in active markets. Master the structure, entry rules, stop placement, and target for each — then only trade the ones that match your plan.
1Bull Flag — Bullish Continuation
What It Is
A sharp, nearly vertical move up on high volume (the pole) followed by a tight, orderly pullback on declining volume (the flag). The pullback drifts sideways or slightly lower in a parallel channel. This is not a reversal — it is energy compressing before the next leg up.
What You Need
1. Strong pole — fast move up on above-average volume. Weak poles = weak breakouts.
2. Tight flag — narrow range, slightly downward sloping. Choppy wide flags are NOT bull flags.
3. Volume dries up during the flag — sellers are not aggressive.
4. Breakout candle closes above the upper channel line on expanding volume.
Entry / Stop / Target
Entry: Break above upper channel line on volume confirmation.
Stop: Below the low of the flag.
Target: Add the pole length to the breakout point.
Common Mistake
Entering during the flag instead of waiting for the breakout. Wait for the break and volume confirmation.
2Bear Flag — Bearish Continuation
What It Is
The mirror of the bull flag. A sharp move down on high volume (the pole) followed by a tight, slightly upward drift on declining volume (the flag). The bounce looks like a recovery — it is not. It is a pause before the next leg down.
What You Need
1. Strong pole down — fast, aggressive selloff on heavy volume.
2. Flag drifts slightly upward on low volume — sellers resting, not retreating.
3. Volume dries up during the drift — buyers have no conviction.
4. Breakdown candle closes below the lower channel line on expanding volume.
Entry / Stop / Target
Entry: Break below the lower channel line on volume.
Stop: Above the high of the flag.
Target: Subtract the pole length from the breakdown point.
3Symmetrical Triangle — Coiling for a Break
What It Is
Price makes lower highs and higher lows, converging into a tighter range. Both buyers and sellers are losing conviction. Volume contracts as the triangle narrows. Eventually one side wins — usually in the direction of the prior trend.
What You Need
1. At least 2 touches on both trendlines — 4 minimum.
2. Volume declining as the triangle compresses.
3. Breakout line closes outside the triangle on expanding volume.
4. Prior trend context matters — a triangle after an uptrend favors an upside break.
Entry / Stop / Target
Entry: Close outside the triangle on volume. Do not enter on a wick.
Stop: Back inside the triangle.
Target: Height of the widest part of the triangle added to the breakout point.
Warning
Symmetrical triangles produce more fakeouts than flags. Volume confirmation is non-negotiable.
4Double Bottom — Reversal From Lows
What It Is
Price tests a support level, bounces, pulls back to test the same level again, and bounces again — forming a W shape. Sellers tried twice to push price lower, failed both times, and buyers are taking control.
What You Need
1. Two distinct lows at approximately the same price level.
2. A clear bounce between the two lows — the middle of the W.
3. Volume often picks up on the second bounce.
4. Pattern is NOT confirmed until price breaks above the neckline.
Entry / Stop / Target
Entry: Break above the neckline on volume.
Stop: Below the second low.
Target: Height of the pattern projected upward from the neckline break.
5Head & Shoulders — Reversal From Highs
What It Is
Three peaks — a left shoulder, a higher head, and a right shoulder roughly equal to the left. The right shoulder fails to reach the head, signaling buyers are losing strength. When price breaks the neckline, the pattern is complete.
What You Need
1. Clear three-peak structure — left shoulder, head, right shoulder.
2. Right shoulder lower than the head — if it exceeds the head, pattern is invalid.
3. Volume highest on left shoulder, declining at head, lowest at right shoulder.
4. Neckline break on increasing volume confirms the reversal.
Entry / Stop / Target
Entry: Break and close below the neckline on volume.
Stop: Above the right shoulder.
Target: Height from head to neckline projected downward from the break.
6Cup & Handle — Bullish Continuation
What It Is
Price sells off gradually forming a smooth, rounded base (the cup) — not a sharp V. After the cup, price pulls back slightly in a tight drift (the handle) on declining volume. When price breaks above the cup high, the move resumes.
What You Need
1. Cup is rounded — gradual, not sharp. Sharp V-bases are not cups.
2. Handle forms in the upper half of the cup — no more than 50% retracement.
3. Volume dries up through the handle.
4. Breakout above the prior high (cup rim) on strong volume.
Entry / Stop / Target
Entry: Break above the cup rim on expanding volume.
Stop: Below the low of the handle.
Target: Depth of the cup added to the breakout point.
Note
The cup & handle typically takes weeks to months to form on daily charts. Do not force it on short timeframes.
