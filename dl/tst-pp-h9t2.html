The Trader's Pattern Playbook
The 6 chart patterns that appear most often with visual examples and exact entry rules
These 6 patterns appear constantly in active markets. Master the structure, entry rules, stop placement, and target for each — then only trade the ones that match your plan.
1Bull Flag — Bullish Continuation
What It Is
A sharp, nearly vertical move up on high volume (the pole) followed by a tight, orderly pullback on declining volume (the flag). The pullback drifts sideways or slightly lower in a parallel channel. This is not a reversal — it is energy compressing before the next leg up.
What You Need
1. Strong pole — fast move up on above-average volume. Weak poles = weak breakouts.
2. Tight flag — narrow range, slightly downward sloping. Choppy wide flags are NOT bull flags.
3. Volume dries up during the flag — sellers are not aggressive.
4. Breakout candle closes above the upper channel line on expanding volume.
Entry / Stop / Target
Entry: Break above upper channel line on volume confirmation — or wait for the first candle to close above it.
Stop: Below the low of the flag (the consolidation low). If it goes there, the setup failed.
Target: Add the pole length to the breakout point. That is your measured move target.
Common Mistake
Entering during the flag instead of waiting for the breakout. You are not buying a discount — you are entering before the setup has confirmed. Wait for the break.
2Bear Flag — Bearish Continuation
What It Is
The exact mirror of the bull flag. A sharp move down on high volume (the pole) followed by a tight, slightly upward drift on declining volume (the flag). The bounce looks like a recovery — it is not. It is a pause before the next leg down.
What You Need
1. Strong pole down — fast, aggressive selloff on heavy volume.
2. Flag drifts slightly upward on low volume — sellers resting, not retreating.
3. Volume dries up during the drift — buyers have no conviction.
4. Breakdown candle closes below the lower channel line on expanding volume.
Entry / Stop / Target
Entry: Break below the lower channel line on volume — or first close below it.
Stop: Above the high of the flag. If the stock reclaims it, the short thesis is wrong.
Target: Subtract the pole length from the breakdown point. That is your measured move target.
3Symmetrical Triangle — Coiling for a Break
What It Is
Price makes lower highs and higher lows, converging into a tighter and tighter range. Both buyers and sellers are losing conviction. Volume contracts as the triangle narrows. Eventually one side wins and price breaks out — usually in the direction of the prior trend.
What You Need
1. At least 2 touches on both the upper and lower trendlines — 4 total minimum.
2. Volume declining as the triangle compresses.
3. Breakout candle closes outside the triangle on expanding volume.
4. Prior trend context — a symmetrical triangle after an uptrend is more likely to break upward.
Entry / Stop / Target
Entry: Candle close outside the triangle on volume. Do not enter on a wick poke — wait for the close.
Stop: Back inside the triangle. A reclaim of the break level means the pattern failed.
Target: Height of the widest part of the triangle added to the breakout point.
Warning
Symmetrical triangles produce more fakeouts than flags. Volume confirmation on the breakout is non-negotiable. Low-volume breaks are suspect until proven otherwise.
4Double Bottom — Reversal From Lows
What It Is
Price tests a support level, bounces, pulls back to test the same level again, and bounces again — forming a W shape. The second low is ideally at or slightly above the first. This pattern signals that sellers tried twice to push price lower, failed both times, and buyers are taking control.
What You Need
1. Two distinct lows at approximately the same price level.
2. A clear bounce between the two lows (the middle of the W).
3. Volume often picks up on the second bounce — buyers stepping in more aggressively.
4. Pattern is NOT confirmed until price breaks above the high between the two lows (the neckline).
Entry / Stop / Target
Entry: Break above the neckline on volume — this is the confirmation. Entering at the second low is higher risk, lower reward.
Stop: Below the second low. If the level breaks, the pattern has failed.
Target: Height of the pattern (neckline to lows) projected upward from the neckline break.
5Head & Shoulders — Reversal From Highs
What It Is
Three peaks — a left shoulder, a higher head, and a right shoulder roughly equal to the left. The right shoulder fails to reach the head, signaling that buyers are losing strength. A neckline connects the lows between the three peaks. When price breaks the neckline, the pattern is complete and the trend has likely reversed.
What You Need
1. Clear three-peak structure — left shoulder, head, right shoulder.
2. Right shoulder lower than the head — critical. If the right shoulder exceeds the head, the pattern is not valid.
3. Volume typically highest on the left shoulder, lower on the head, lower still on the right shoulder — declining participation on each rally.
4. Neckline break on increasing volume confirms the reversal.
Entry / Stop / Target
Entry: Break and close below the neckline on volume. Many traders wait for a retest of the neckline from below (former support becomes resistance) for a lower-risk entry.
Stop: Above the right shoulder.
Target: Height from head to neckline projected downward from the neckline break.
6Cup & Handle — Bullish Continuation
What It Is
Price sells off gradually and forms a smooth, rounded base (the cup) — not a sharp V-shape. After the cup, price pulls back slightly in a tight, orderly drift (the handle) on declining volume. The handle forms in the upper half of the cup. When price breaks above the cup high, the move resumes.
What You Need
1. Cup is rounded — gradual, not sharp. Sharp V-bases are not cups.
2. Handle forms in the upper half of the cup — if it dips more than 50% back into the cup, the pattern weakens.
3. Volume dries up through the handle.
4. Breakout above the prior high (the cup rim) on strong volume.
Entry / Stop / Target
Entry: Break above the cup rim on expanding volume.
Stop: Below the low of the handle.
Target: Depth of the cup added to the breakout point.
Key Distinction
The cup & handle is a longer-term pattern — it typically takes weeks to months to form properly on daily charts. Do not force it on short timeframes where the pattern has no meaning.
