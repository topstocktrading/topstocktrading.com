What the PMIs Said

The S&P Global Flash PMI data for September dropped Wednesday morning at 9:45 AM — and the numbers came in hotter than expected across the board. Services PMI hit 57.3 against an estimate of 55.7. Manufacturing came in at 54.8 against a 53.5 estimate. The composite reading jumped to 56.4.

For context: any reading above 50 signals expansion. Above 55 is considered strong growth. At 57.3, the services sector is running well above what most economists had forecast for the month after the Fed's rate hike last week.

Why These Numbers Matter So Much Right Now

The PMIs are the first major economic data point for September — which means they're the first real look at how the economy responded to the Fed's rate hike cycle heading into this month. The question the market needed answered: is the economy starting to cool, or is it still running hot enough to justify more tightening?

The answer was unambiguous. The economy is not cooling. Consumer spending on services is accelerating, manufacturing is expanding, and input prices — noted in the report — are rising again. That last point is the one that will keep the Fed up at night.

The Market Read

Within minutes of the print, the 10-year Treasury yield spiked. QQQ fell 1.3%. TLT dropped 0.8%. The dollar strengthened. This is the classic "hot data = risk off" playbook. The market repriced the October hike probability to 68% almost instantly.

What Comes Next

The October FOMC meeting is now very much live. With services PMI this strong, the Fed has all the justification it needs to hike again. The path of least resistance for yields is higher, which means the path of least resistance for growth stocks is lower — at least until the data turns.

Key things to watch between now and October:

  • Jobs report — a strong number confirms the hot PMI narrative
  • CPI — if inflation re-accelerates, all bets are off on a pause
  • 10-year yield — approaching the critical 5% level. A break there would be significant
  • Consumer spending data — Costco earnings Thursday will be an important real-world check
