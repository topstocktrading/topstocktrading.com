Candlestick Basics
Read any candlestick chart with confidence
Every candle tells a story about who won the battle between buyers and sellers during a specific time period. Once you understand how to read individual candles and candlestick patterns, you can make faster, more confident decisions.
1Anatomy of a Candle
PartWhat it represents
BodyThe range between the open and close price. Wide body = strong conviction.
Upper WickHighest price reached. Sellers pushed price back down from the high.
Lower WickLowest price reached. Buyers pushed price back up from the low.
Green candleClose was higher than open. Buyers won the period.
Red candleClose was lower than open. Sellers won the period.
2Key Single Candle Signals
CandleWhat to look forSignal
MarubozuLarge body, no wicks — open is high/low, close is oppositeStrong directional conviction
DojiOpen and close nearly the same — indecisionPotential reversal — wait for next candle
HammerSmall body at top, long lower wick (2x body minimum)Bullish reversal at support
Shooting StarSmall body at bottom, long upper wickBearish reversal at resistance
Spinning TopSmall body, wicks on both sidesIndecision — no clear winner
EngulfingLarge candle completely covers prior candle's bodyStrong reversal signal
3Reading Wicks
Long upper wick = rejection at high
Sellers came in aggressively and pushed price back down. The high was rejected. Bearish signal especially at resistance.
Long lower wick = rejection at low
Buyers came in aggressively and pushed price back up. The low was rejected. Bullish signal especially at support.
No wick = strong close
Price moved in one direction and closed at the extreme. Strongest signal of conviction — especially on breakouts.
4Timeframes Matter
The same pattern means different things on different timeframes
A hammer on a 1-minute chart is noise. A hammer on a daily chart at major support is a significant signal. Always consider the timeframe context before acting on a candle signal.
